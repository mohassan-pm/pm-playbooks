# Enterprise growth playbook

*A playbook for high-touch, B2B SaaS growth, built from what I've seen work in the field when it comes to delivering enterprise features for some of the world's largest enterprises*

---

> **Before you read!**
> 
> This playbook assumes the product foundations are in place and the product is already loved and used by many people and that has pivoted to penetrate the enterprise segment, or for teams that are going after enterprise from the start. If that is not your situation, you probably do not need this. Your traditional PLG playbook is all you need.

---

## 00. Strategy alignment comes first

Before you set up any feedback loop, you need to agree on who you are going after. If product and GTM are not aligned on the target segment, the feedback you collect will come from the wrong cohorts. You will end up building for customers you are not trying to win, and optimising for deals that do not represent where you want to go.

> This is the foundation for everything that follows, especially step 01. Without alignment on strategy, your feedback loops will generate noise instead of signal. Alignment is what determines which feedback is worth acting on and which should be ignored, even if it is loud.

🎯 **Pick a segment, win it** 

Align product and GTM on a single geo or industry before expanding. Concentrated effort produces concentrated, useful feedback and gives you a real shot at winning. Spreading too thin means you get weak signal from everywhere and strong results from nowhere.

📝 **Document the Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) explicitly.** 

Define the target customer by industry, size, geography, and maturity level. When product and GTM share the same written definition, conversations about priorities stop being arguments about opinions.

🚀 **Use wins to expand** 

Once you have won a segment, moving into adjacent geos or industries becomes much easier. The ICP is validated, the playbook is documented, and you have case studies to point to.

⚠️ **Misalignment is the silent killer** 

When product and GTM are talking to different customers, the feedback conflicts and roadmap decisions get made on bad signals. By the time you notice, you have wasted quarters going in different directions. Alignment is not a nice-to-have, it is the starting point.

## 01. Establish feedback loops with sales and GTM

After getting alignment in place, this is the next thing to prioritise above everything else, if you do not have this in place today, it is worth stopping everything else and setting it up first. Without a structured feedback loop between product and GTM, prioritisation becomes guesswork. Sales and product gradually drift toward solving different problems, and by the time you notice, deals are lost and nobody can clearly explain why.

🗂️ **Build a structured tracking system for enterprise blockers** 

Every friction point that surfaces in a sales or CS conversation needs to be tracked. The way we approached this was through Jira tickets, where each ticket represents a feature request with dropdowns and labels to connect it to specific deals and deal sizes, Atlassian Assets app is also a good option that offers APIs to integrate with you CRM systems . In addition to this, it really matters to be explicit about the type of blocker: is it a sales blocker, meaning it is preventing a deal from closing, or an adoption blocker, meaning the customer has signed but is struggling to get value. The distinction changes both the urgency and the response.

🔍 **Win/loss deal analysis** 

Hopefully your sales teams are adding notes once they close deals, if not please ask them to do so urgently! Go beyond those notes and talk directly to AEs and customers. When you look across 5 to 10 deals together, patterns start to emerge that are far more useful than short notes.

🤖 **Use AI agents to mine unstructured data** 

Many sales orgs record their customer interactions on Loom, Zoom, or Gong, and all of these tools can generate transcripts. You can build an agent that goes through those transcripts, extracts the common themes, and either reports them back or creates and updates the tickets you are already using to track blockers. This turns hours of recordings into structured product signal with very little manual effort.

🗺️ **Segment-aware requirement mapping** 

Different geos and industries have different compliance thresholds. A healthcare customer in the US has different requirements than a fintech in the EU. Tooling that maps requirements to segments gives you a clear view of where your product already meets the bar and where the gaps are. This is also a useful strategic input, as it helps you identify which segment pivot is the most achievable and can inform broader strategy shifts before they are made.

📅 **Quarterly or biannual friction reports** 

The right cadence depends on the length of your sales cycles and how fast the product is moving. Longer enterprise cycles with slower product changes can work on a biannual basis. The goal is to keep the signal current without turning it into a reporting exercise nobody has time for.

🔁 **Close the loop with sales, and do not underestimate this part** 

When a blocker that was logged actually gets shipped, communicate it to the field teams quickly. What I have seen is that customers are genuinely impressed when they find out that something they raised during the sales process was actually addressed. It builds a level of trust that is hard to create any other way, and it is usually more impactful than most people expect.

📣 **Make readiness part of your GTM story** 

Once your compliance posture is solid, it should be communicated actively. Work with your GTM team to build compliance into the sales narrative early, include in any conference keynote sessions, reference certifications in outbound materials, and equip field teams to speak confidently about them.

---

> 🤔 **Doesn't this go against the conventional wisdom of getting feedback directly from users?**
> Not really. The challenge in enterprise is not that you cannot talk to users. It is that the people who choose the product are usually not the people who will use it, so you have to get past that layer first. Once you have identified a feature worth building from your GTM signals, that is the right moment to go deep on user research. Talk to actual users, understand the problem properly, and build something that genuinely solves it. This pays off in two ways. When you eventually get past the Request for Proposal (RFP) or Request For Information (RFI) stages and are in the room showing the product to the people who will use it day to day, they can tell it was built with them in mind. And when renewal conversations come around, you are not defending your value, you are just pointing to a track record of listening and delivering.

## 02. Setting the right OKRs for enterprise growth

OKRs in enterprise are genuinely hard to get right. The true north metric is clear: seat growth. But the nature of enterprise sales cycles means that metric is always lagging, sometimes by quarters. This creates pressure to measure the wrong things, and that pressure can quietly damage the product and the team if you are not careful.

🎯 **Seat growth is your true metric, but it is a lagging one**

The most accurate measure of enterprise growth is seats, how many users inside a customer account are actively using the product. It reflects real adoption, real expansion, and real retention. The problem is that enterprise sales cycles are long and adoption takes time, so seat growth moves slowly. It should always be the metric everything else is pointing toward, even when it is hard to move quickly.

⚠️ **Delivery-based OKRs are tempting, and extremely dangerous**

When seat growth is too lagging to show up in a quarterly review, and when team morale and performance reviews are on the line, there is a very natural pull toward delivery-based key results. Shipped features feel like progress. The problem is that delivery OKRs incentivise teams to build things and move on, with no real accountability for whether what was built actually works or gets used. This can help you tick boxes in an RFP or an RFI, but it will fall apart the moment you are in a live demo or a customer tries to use the product for real.

🔄 **If you have to start with delivery KRs, pivot out of them as fast as you can**

Sometimes the product genuinely lacks the foundations, and delivery-based KRs are a necessary starting point. Treat them as a temporary goal. As soon as the core foundations are in place, move to engagement and usage metrics. These are early signals of the thing you actually care about. Use them as a leading indicator of seat growth and be transparent with your team and stakeholders about why you are making the switch.

> 🚨 **The pattern to watch for**
>
> Teams running delivery OKRs for too long tend to ship a lot and grow slowly. The backlog looks healthy, the roadmap looks full, but the customer problems are not solved, the sales team keeps hitting the same objections and customers churn quietly. If this sounds familiar, the OKR structure is probably part of the problem.

## 03. Migrations as a growth weapon, not just a utility

Migration tooling is typically framed as a switching cost reducer. That undersells it. Enterprise customers almost always have something in place already, whether that is a proper tool, a set of spreadsheets, or at the very least their users and teams managed through an active directory or similar systems. This is actually an advantage. A well-designed migration experience gives your sales team something most competitors cannot offer: the ability to show up with the customer's own data, in your product, on day one of the conversation.

📊 **Demo on real customer data** 

Sales teams can use migration tooling to import a prospect's actual data and run live demos in your product. Prospects see their own workflows, their own teams, their own history, not generic templates. That shift alone changes the tone of the conversation from "this looks interesting" to "this could actually work for us".

💡 **Show them problems they did not know they had**

This is not just an outcome of the migration process. Combine what the migration surfaces with your in-product analytics, or even third party analytics tools, to give customers a view of their own operations they have never had before. Process bottlenecks, team workload imbalances, things that are hard to see when you are deep in the day to day. Enterprise customers pay consulting firms significant fees to get this kind of organisational insight. When your field team shows up as consultants rather than sellers, armed with real data about the customer's own environment, they will win deals that a standard demo never would. From a product perspective, this means prioritising migration tooling and in-product value demonstration features like analytics dashboards as first class citizens, not afterthoughts.

⚡ **Reduce time-to-value** 

Getting a customer to their first win faster than the competition wins the renewal conversation before it starts. Migration tooling that removes the blank-slate problem is what makes that possible.

## 04. Champion development as a growth motion

Enterprise deals rely heavily on internal champions. The PM's job is to work closely with field and GTM teams to equip those champions with everything they need to sell the product upward inside their org.

> **What is a champion?** A champion is someone inside the prospective customer's organisation who believes in your product and is willing to advocate for it internally. They are usually not the final decision maker, but they are the person doing the work of convincing the people who are. They attend your demos, they share your materials internally, and they fight your corner in rooms you will never be in. Without a champion, even a great product can lose a deal.

🛠️ **Build champion kits together with your field and GTM teams** 

Work with sales and CS to understand what objections champions are facing internally, then build the tools that help them respond. This includes ROI calculators that show not just the return but also the break-even point, so champions can answer the question of how long before this pays for itself. Add before/after metrics templates and internal pitch decks they can take directly into leadership conversations. The closer these materials are to what champions are actually being asked to justify, the more useful they will be.

📈 **Measure what champions care about**

A champion's success metric is rarely the same as the end user's. They are accountable to business outcomes, not feature adoption. Build reporting that speaks that language, time saved, cost reduced, risk mitigated, so champions always have something concrete to point to when they are asked to justify the investment.

---

> **A note on the product work involved here.** This section may not require a lot of in-product development. The real work is coordination. That said, one area where product and engineering can add significant value is making it easy to extract the right data from the product to feed into these ROI calculators. If a champion needs to show how many hours their team saved or how much faster requests are being resolved, that data needs to be accessible and reliable.

## 05. Getting customers to value, fast

Time-to-value is critical in building enterprise trust. Enterprise customers have signed a significant contract and their leadership is watching. Getting them to an early, visible win is not just good onboarding, it is the first real signal to the business that the ROI they were promised is actually going to materialise. The faster you can deliver that signal, the stronger the trust and the easier your renewal conversations become.

🧩 **Eliminate the blank-slate problem** 

Base your onboarding on the customer's industry, prior tool or templates to start from. The less work a customer has to do before seeing value, the faster the trust builds.

✨ **Define the aha moment per segment** 

The first win for a financial services customer looks different from a software team's. Tailor the onboarding journey to surface it faster for each segment you are targeting.

📉 **Adjust your retention metrics to account for Time To Value (TTV)**

Retention data alone can be misleading. Pair it with TTV and you get a much clearer picture of where onboarding is actually working.

## 06. Tooling that makes enterprise readiness visible

Enterprise readiness is often invisible until a deal is already at risk. The right internal tooling surfaces gaps early and turns compliance from a blocker into a selling point.

🖥️ **Enterprise posture dashboard** 

A single view that tracks every system component and its compliance state against each enterprise requirement. SSO, audit logs, data residency, and admin controls, each one mapped to its current status across the product.

🔎 **Auto-detect compliance gaps** 

Tooling that continuously scans the product against a defined compliance checklist and flags regressions automatically, this helps avoid the need of high frequenct audits. Catching non-compliance proactively is far cheaper than discovering it during a security review with a large deal on the line.


🔐 **Build a trust centre** 

A dedicated page or portal where enterprise buyers can self-serve compliance documentation, certifications, and audit reports. This removes a very common late-stage friction point and signals to prospects that you take security seriously enough to make it easy to verify. The less back-and-forth required during a security review, the faster the deal moves.
